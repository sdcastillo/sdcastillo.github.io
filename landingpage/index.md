**Updated:** 2026-10-19 08:07 EDT

Based on the latest forecasts and polling averages available, here is the outlook for the **2026 United States Midterm Elections**:

### **House of Representatives (435 seats)**
The **Democratic Party** is widely favored to win a majority.

- **Projected seat range:** Multiple forecasters project Democrats winning roughly **220–235 seats**, compared to Republicans winning roughly **200–215 seats**.
- **Key forecasts:**
  - **Decision Desk HQ:** Projects Democrats at **235 seats** to Republicans’ 200.
  - **Cornell University model:** Anticipates Democrats winning approximately **226 seats** to Republicans’ 209 (with 8 in 10 odds of a Democratic majority).
  - **Cook Political Report:** Forecasts a Democratic net gain of **9 to 19 seats**, which would put them in the range of roughly 223–233 seats.
  - **Fox News:** Projects Democrats at **at least 219 seats** (enough for a majority), with Republicans at 198 and 18 toss-ups.
- **Threshold:** 218 seats are needed for a majority. Republicans currently hold a slim majority (218 seats to Democrats’ 214, plus vacancies and one Independent).

### **Senate (35 seats up; 100 total)**
The Senate is a much closer contest, though forecasting models and prediction markets lean slightly Democratic.

- **Current breakdown:** Republicans hold 53 seats; Democrats (including two Independents who caucus with them) hold 47.
- **Projected outcome:**
  - **Cook Political Report consensus forecast:** roughly **48 Democrats / 47 Republicans** (with 2 Independents), meaning Democrats would fall just short of the 51 seats needed for control (or 50 with the Vice President’s tie-breaking vote).
  - **Prediction markets:** Polymarket and Kalshi give Democrats roughly a **60–65% chance** of winning Senate control.
  - **Decision Desk HQ:** Gives Democrats a 56% chance, projecting Republicans to finish with 49 seats.
- **Threshold:** Democrats need a net gain of **4 seats** to win control (or 3 if they hold the Vice Presidency, which they do under JD Vance, so they would need 51 seats outright or 50 with a tie-break—however, with a Republican VP, Democrats need 51 to control the chamber).

### **Bottom Line**
- **House:** Democrats are the clear favorite, likely winning **around 225–235 seats**.
- **Senate:** Too close to call with high confidence, but slightly favors Democrats; most consensus forecasts project a near tie (roughly 48–50 seats each), with control potentially hinging on a handful of toss-up races.

*Note: These are forecasts based on polling and ratings as of October 2026. Actual results will depend on voter turnout, late-breaking events, and individual race outcomes on Election Day (November 3, 2026).*
