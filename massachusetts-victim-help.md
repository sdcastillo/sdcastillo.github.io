If you are in immediate danger, call **911**.

This page lists public offices and nonprofits that help crime victims and survivors in Massachusetts. It is general information gathered from those organizations' own pages on October 12, 2026. It is not legal advice. Hours and phone numbers change, so confirm them on the linked page before you rely on them.

## A lawyer who represents you

The [Victim Rights Law Center](https://victimrights.org/how-vrlc-can-help/) (VRLC) gives free legal help in Massachusetts to people who have experienced sexual assault, and to people who have experienced stalking when the stalking has a sexual component. You do not have to report to the police to ask them for help. They do not take walk-ins. Meetings are by appointment. Office hours are Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., with other times by appointment.

- **Address:** 11 Beacon Street, Suite 520, Boston, MA 02108
- **Legal help email:** [legalhelp@victimrights.org](mailto:legalhelp@victimrights.org)
- **Other questions:** [vrlc@victimrights.org](mailto:vrlc@victimrights.org)
- **Phone:** (617) 399-6720, intake extension 19
- **Languages listed by Mass.gov:** English and Spanish
- **Official listings:** [victimrights.org](https://www.victimrights.org/) and the [Mass.gov location page](https://www.mass.gov/locations/victim-rights-law-center)

VRLC says its lawyers may be able to help with privacy, safety, K-12 school, college, housing, work, money and public benefits, a criminal case, and help for children and teens. Mass.gov also lists help with immigration status. On its site, VRLC describes two Massachusetts court orders:

1. A **209A abuse prevention order**, for harm by someone you live with, a family member, or an intimate partner.
2. A **258E harassment prevention order**, for harm, stalking, or harassment by someone else.

VRLC also says a restraining order does not guarantee that the other person will stay away, and that safety planning can sit alongside a court order.

VRLC draws a sharp line between its lawyers and the people who work on a criminal case:

- The **district attorney** represents the Commonwealth. The district attorney is not the victim's lawyer. What a victim tells the district attorney is not confidential, and it may be shared with the defense.
- A **victim-witness advocate** works for the district attorney's office. What a victim tells that advocate is not confidential.
- A **police report** can include what a victim tells an officer, and that report can be shared with the district attorney and with the person accused.
- A person does not have to speak, outside of court, with the defense attorney or the defense investigator.
- A **VRLC attorney** represents the survivor. VRLC says a child's one-on-one conversations with their own lawyer are confidential, a child can call without first telling a parent, and Massachusetts lawyers are not mandated reporters. School decisions can still require a parent or guardian.

## Rights while a criminal case is open

The Massachusetts Victim Bill of Rights is [Massachusetts General Laws chapter 258B](https://malegislature.gov/Laws/GeneralLaws/PartIII/TitleIV/Chapter258B). It gives crime victims rights to be informed, present, and heard. Victim compensation is in chapter 258C. Witness protection is in chapter 263A. For federal cases, crime victims' rights are in [18 U.S.C. § 3771](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/18/3771). The Trial Court Law Library keeps the statute list, regulations, and related guides on its [victims and witnesses page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/massachusetts-law-about-victims-and-witnesses-of-crimes). Law librarians can be reached at [lawlibraries@jud.state.ma.us](mailto:lawlibraries@jud.state.ma.us).

[MOVA](https://www.mass.gov/orgs/massachusetts-office-for-victim-assistance) is the state agency for victim rights and services. Boston office: 1 Ashburton Place, Suite 1310, Boston, MA 02108. General email: [MOVA@mass.gov](mailto:MOVA@mass.gov). Compensation correspondence: [VCCorrespondence@mass.gov](mailto:VCCorrespondence@mass.gov). The Trial Court law-library page links to the Attorney General's victims of violent crime compensation program.

Victim-witness advocates at a district attorney's office help with court dates, protective orders, victim-impact statements, and compensation applications. They are part of the prosecution team, so the confidentiality limit above still applies. The [Cape and Islands District Attorney](https://capeandislandsda.gov/101/Victim-Witness-Services) says these services are free in every court in that district, and that advocates can also explain bail notice and the CJIS Victim Notification Registry, which notifies a victim if a defendant is released or escapes. That office lists these community referrals: Children's Cove, Housing Assistance Corporation, Independence House, Safe Plan, and the WE CAN Center.

Every other Massachusetts district attorney's office has its own victim-witness unit. AskMOVA or the phone number for that county's district attorney is the way to reach the unit where the case is pending.

## Free legal help, and how to find a private lawyer

[Mass.gov's finding-legal-help page](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/finding-legal-help) says a state agency cannot act as your personal lawyer. These are the free and referral numbers printed there.

- [Civil Legal Aid for Victims of Crime (CLAVC)](https://www.massclavc.org/) and the [Massachusetts Legal Resource Finder](https://masslrf.org/). CLAVC routes people to the legal-aid program for their town.
- Massachusetts Legal Assistance Corporation's crime-victim partners are listed by the federal court at 7 Winthrop Square, 2nd Floor, Boston, MA 02112, website [massclavc.org](https://www.massclavc.org/).

Commercial directories mix many kinds of law practices. A listing labeled "domestic violence" on a private directory can be a lawyer who defends people accused of that conduct. That is a different service from a victim advocate, SafeLink, MOVA, legal aid, or VRLC. Start with the offices above when the goal is help for a victim or survivor.
